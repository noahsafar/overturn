# Ideal Customer Profile (Phase 1 pilot)

Per the master plan we want **independent specialty practices, 5-50 providers,
behavioral health or PT, currently outsourcing billing.** This doc makes that
specific enough you can spot one in a directory in 10 seconds.

## Hard filters (disqualify if any of these is wrong)

- **Practice size**: 5-50 providers. Smaller = denial volume too low to matter. Larger = procurement gauntlet.
- **Specialty**: Behavioral health (psychiatry, psychology, LCSW practices) OR physical therapy. Reasons:
  - Denial-heavy specialties (low billing-team density per provider).
  - Procedure codes are stable, payer policies are public, easier to wedge.
  - Avoid first: oncology (high stakes), primary care (margins too thin).
- **Ownership**: Independent, NOT owned by a hospital system. Hospital-owned = procurement = no.
- **EHR**: NOT Epic. They run on Athena / DrChrono / eCW / Kareo / AdvancedMD / SimplePractice / TheraNest. Epic-shop practices are uniformly hospital-tethered.
- **Currently outsources billing OR has 1-2 in-house billers**. If they have a full 5-person billing department they've already invested — harder sell. If they outsource (paying 4-8% of collections to a billing co), you're saving them 25% of what they thought was already lost.

## Soft signals (the gold)

- They mention "denial management" or "AR cleanup" on their website's careers page.
- They've posted a billing-manager job opening in the last 6 months.
- The practice owner is on LinkedIn and posts about staffing struggles.
- They've recently switched billing companies (search Yelp / Google reviews for billing-related complaints).
- The practice is run by a millennial-or-younger physician owner (more willing to try new tech).
- They're a CT-licensed practice (your Yale geographic anchor).

## Decision-maker map

| Title | Real interest | What they care about |
|---|---|---|
| **Owner-physician** | Their P&L | "Will this make me money? Will it make me look bad?" |
| **Practice manager / administrator** | Day-to-day pain | "Will this make my biller's life easier or harder? Will I have to retrain anyone?" |
| **Office manager** (small practices) | Same as practice manager | Tends to be the same person as the bookkeeper. |
| **Billing manager** | Their own job | THREAT: "is this AI going to replace me?" — frame as "this is the work you don't get to anyway." |
| **CFO** (larger practices, 20+ providers) | Cash flow | Loves outcomes-based pricing. Easy yes if you can prove ROI. |

**Sequence**: for practices 5-15 providers, go straight to owner-physician or practice manager.
For 15-50 providers, start with the office/practice manager — owner won't take a cold meeting but will join a follow-up after the manager filters you in.

## Anti-personas (skip)

- Anyone who works at a hospital, even a small one
- Telehealth-only startups (they already use modern RCM tooling)
- Solo practitioners (their denial volume is ~50/year — not worth their time or yours)
- Practices that just signed a 12-month billing-co contract (they'll wait it out)

## Geographic anchor

- **Primary**: New Haven CT + Hartford CT + surrounding (Yale proximity, drivable, small ecosystem).
- **Secondary**: Boston metro (1.5h train, big behavioral-health density, alumni network).
- **Tertiary**: NYC metro (huge market, less efficient outreach).
- **Don't bother yet**: Bay Area / LA — competitors are thicker there.

## The one-sentence test

If you can't answer "yes" to this in 30 seconds of looking at a practice, skip:

> *"Could this practice realistically have ≥500 denied claims/year that nobody works?"*

500 denials × $200 avg = $100K of unworked revenue. Your 25% = $25K of fees from one practice. That's a viable pilot.

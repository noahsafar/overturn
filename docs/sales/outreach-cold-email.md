# Cold email templates

Three variants. Match to the persona. Send from your @yale.edu address.

Subject-line discipline: no clickbait, no exclamation points, no "Quick question"
spam tropes. Two patterns work: (a) state the value as a noun phrase, (b) state
who's writing. Both are below.

## Variant A — to a practice owner-physician (small practice, 5-15 providers)

**Subject**: Yale student building denial-recovery tooling — 15 min?

> Dr. [Last Name],
>
> I'm a Yale student building Overturn — software that automates appeals
> on denied insurance claims for small specialty practices. We draft the
> appeal letter, verify every citation against the payer's published policies,
> get a human at your practice to approve, then submit through the portal.
>
> I'd love 15 minutes to learn how your practice handles denials today
> — what your biller actually does with them, which ones get worked,
> which ones get written off. I'm trying to understand whether the
> problem we've built for is the problem you actually have.
>
> No pitch, no slides. Genuine research call. I can come to your office
> in [city] or do it over Zoom — your call.
>
> Available windows next week: [3-4 options].
>
> Thanks,
> Noah Safar
> Yale '[year] · [phone]

**Why this works:**
- Yale + student framing in the first line clears spam filters and signals "no risk to take this meeting."
- Doesn't pitch — asks for their input. People say yes to "tell me about your work" more than "let me sell you something."
- Specific time options force a decision instead of "let me know when works."
- 15 min, not 30 — lower commitment ask.

## Variant B — to a practice manager / administrator (any size)

**Subject**: How does [Practice Name] work denied claims?

> Hi [First Name],
>
> Saw on LinkedIn you run operations at [Practice Name]. I'm a Yale
> student building Overturn — AI agents that draft + submit appeals
> on denied claims, on behalf of small specialty practices. Outcomes-based
> pricing, no setup fees.
>
> I'm trying to learn how practices like yours handle denial follow-up
> today — what gets worked vs written off, who does the work, what makes
> it slow. Would you have 15-20 minutes for a research call? I'm not
> pitching anything yet, just trying to understand the problem from
> people who live it.
>
> Some times next week: [3 options]. Happy to be flexible.
>
> Thanks,
> Noah · Yale '[year]
> [phone] · [linkedin]

**Why this works:**
- Practice managers see "research call" as low-risk and ego-flattering ("come learn from me").
- Specifies "small specialty practices" so they immediately know if they're the audience.
- Names a concrete number (15-20 min). Half hour is the magic threshold above which calendars say no.

## Variant C — to a behavioral-health-specific owner

Use this when you can mention something specific from the practice's website
(specialty focus, recent expansion, founder background). Even one personalized
detail raises reply rates ~3-4×.

**Subject**: New Haven behavioral health + denied claims — Yale student question

> Dr. [Last Name],
>
> Noticed [Practice Name] has been growing — [specific thing: e.g.,
> "expanded to 8 clinicians" / "added the IOP program" / "added the
> Hamden location"]. As you've scaled, I'm guessing the denial follow-up
> burden has scaled with you.
>
> I'm a Yale student building Overturn — software that handles appeals
> on denied behavioral-health claims end to end (drafting against the
> actual payer policy, citing your chart notes, submitting through the
> portal). It's outcomes-based: practice pays nothing unless we recover
> money you'd already written off.
>
> I'm not pitching yet — I want to understand how your team handles
> denials today before I tell you whether what we've built actually
> solves your problem. 15 minutes next week?
>
> Available: [3 options].
>
> Thanks,
> Noah · Yale '[year]
> [phone]

**Why this works:**
- The opening line shows you did 30 seconds of homework. Massive trust signal.
- "I want to understand … before I tell you whether what we've built actually solves your problem" inverts the usual sales script. Most founders pitch first and ask later; you ask first.

## What NOT to do (pattern-match these and don't)

- "Quick question" subject lines — pure spam signal.
- "I'd love to pick your brain" — feels extractive.
- Multi-paragraph backstory about you. They don't care yet.
- Calendly links in the first email. Offer specific times instead. Calendly = "click here to bow to my schedule."
- Mentioning a "$262B problem in healthcare." They live in it; they don't need you to tell them how big it is.
- Attaching a deck. You haven't earned the deck-open yet.

## Follow-up cadence (without being a pest)

- **Day 0**: Email 1 (variant matched to persona).
- **Day 4** (mid-morning Tuesday): Email 2 — short, 2 sentences. See `followup-templates.md` template "F1".
- **Day 11** (mid-morning Tuesday): Email 3 — final touch. See template "F2".
- After Email 3: stop. Move on. They're not interested or got busy. You can re-engage in 3 months with a "what's new" note.

Reply rate on a clean cold list, with Yale signaling + persona-matched variant: **8-15%**. So 30 emails ≈ 3-5 replies ≈ 2-3 calls booked.
